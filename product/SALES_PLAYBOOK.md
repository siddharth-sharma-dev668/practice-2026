# Sales Playbook — how to actually increase sales

Simple model, real numbers, and a decision tree so you always know which one thing to fix next.

---

## 1. The funnel (only four numbers matter)

```
   Reach            people who saw a post
     ↓  ~2–5%
   Profile visits   people curious enough to tap your name
     ↓  ~5–15%
   Link clicks      people who tapped the link in bio
     ↓  ~2–8%
   Sales            people who paid $19
```

Multiply it through. To get **10 sales/month** at mid-range rates you need roughly:

`10 sales ÷ 4% = 250 link clicks ÷ 10% = 2,500 profile visits ÷ 3% = ~83,000 reach/month`

That is **~7,000 reach per post** at 12 posts/month. A new account gets 200–800. **This is why months 1–2 produce almost nothing.**

**Treat those percentages as placeholders, not benchmarks.** The research went looking for a real conversion rate for a sub-$30 digital product sold from a cold social account and could not find one. Anyone who quotes you a number for this has made it up. Your own first six rows of the table below are the only trustworthy figures you will ever have here — which is exactly why filling it in matters more than reading it.

**The shortcut past the maths:** conversations convert far better than a link in bio. Twenty real conversations beat 2,000 passive impressions at your size. In month 1, talking to people *is* the growth strategy — but **comment-to-DM only, never cold DMs.** Meta prohibits messaging users who have not interacted with you first, and it is a documented account-restriction trigger.

**Where the numbers live:**
- Impressions, profile views → **LinkedIn post analytics** (your primary channel) — under each post, and in Analytics on your profile
- Reach, profile visits, saves → Instagram **Professional Dashboard → Insights** (secondary channel, same assets)
- Link clicks → **one UTM per channel**, so you can tell LinkedIn from Instagram from Reddit. Set this up before your first post or the data is unattributable forever
- Sales, refunds, conversion → your payment rail's analytics

**Only three numbers actually matter weekly:** impressions → link clicks → checkout starts. Everything else is decoration until one of those three moves.

**The two metrics most people ignore, which predict everything:**
- **Saves** — the strongest signal that content is genuinely useful. High saves means the algorithm will keep pushing that post. Track it per post.
- **Sales conversion on the product page** (Gumroad shows views → sales). Under 2% means your *page* is the problem, not your traffic.

## 3. The decision tree — what to fix next

Work top to bottom. **Fix one thing at a time**, then wait two weeks. Changing three things at once teaches you nothing.

**Reach is low (< 500/post) →** the content is the problem, not the product.
→ Rewrite your first lines; the hook is most of your reach. Comment substantively on 5 larger accounts daily. Do **not** respond by adding a Reels pipeline — at 3 h/week, video production cost is what breaks the budget, and the Instagram-specific format evidence did not survive verification.

**The gate that overrides everything below.** If impressions-to-link-clicks stays under **0.5% for four consecutive weeks**, stop tuning copy and change *who you are talking to*. Wrong audience looks exactly like bad copy from the inside, and this is the cheapest way to tell them apart. This gate exists because the LinkedIn-primary recommendation is a reasoned bet, not a measurement — and one documented case had 3×-weekly LinkedIn posting produce zero sales across four products, with the seller concluding *"LinkedIn is a viewing audience, not a buying audience."* You want to find that out in eight weeks, not eight months.

**Reach is fine but profile visits are low →** your hook lands but your identity does not.
→ Your posts are not making people curious about *you*. Add a line of personal stake ("I failed this"). Make sure your name/handle is visible on the last slide of every carousel.

**Profile visits are fine but link clicks are low →** your bio is not doing its job.
→ Bio must say what you give and for whom, in one line, plus an explicit "↓" cue. Put the offer in the pinned post too. Never make people hunt.

**Link clicks are fine but sales are low (< 2%) →** the sales page is the problem.
→ Rewrite the top of the page (§5). Add real testimonials. Show 3 actual screenshots. Make the price and the "runs offline, your resume never leaves your device" line impossible to miss.

**Sales happen but refunds are high (> 5%) →** you over-promised.
→ Tighten the page to describe exactly what it is. Remove any implication of a guaranteed job or a guaranteed timeline.

## 4. Levers, in order of return per hour

1. **Sell your time next to the file.** A ₹999–1,499 session out-earns a plausible month of app sales in your first quarter, and it is the only thing on your shelf a free tool cannot clone. 90 min setup.
2. **Talk to the right person.** The documented failure mode here is selling to peers — one post-mortem turned 130k+ Reddit views into 55 store visits and single-digit sales, diagnosed as *"they're my peers, not my buyers."* Your buyer is a 0–3 YOE engineer at a service company or a final-year student who has never heard of Grind 75. This costs zero hours; it is a decision.
3. **Reply to every comment and DM with a real sentence.** Highest-converting activity available at your size, and it is the first thing that gets skipped. 20 min/week.
4. **Publish weekly under your own name for a long time.** Nothing else compounds, and nothing substitutes. Both zero-to-audience successes in this niche published for 1–3 years before the paid product landed.
5. **Make the first line of every post better.** Same content, several times the reach.
6. **Get 5–8 named reviews via a disclosed beta.** Free copies for real job-seekers, written permission, labelled *"received a free copy in exchange for an honest review."* Never write or edit a testimonial — the FTC consumer-reviews rule (effective 21 Oct 2024) bans fabricated and incentivised-positive reviews.
7. **Build the email list.** The only audience you own. One useful email a week, no pitch.
8. **Pin your best free thing.** The diagnostic carousel does your selling for you.
9. **Show the product working in 15 seconds**, ending on the cut list — that is the part nothing free does.

**Deliberately NOT on this list:**
- **Paid ads** — not until the page converts over 3% organically, or you are paying to amplify a leak.
- **A share-button row.** Three independent datasets put share-button click-through at **0.2–0.25%** of visitors (GOV.UK over 6.8M pageviews; Moovweb over 61M mobile sessions; Wroblewski over 18M). People screenshot and copy-paste instead.
- **A checkout upsell at launch.** The earlier draft recommended this; with zero social proof, added choice raises abandonment. Mention the session in the post-purchase email instead.
- **A/B testing anything.** ~10,000 visitors per variant to resolve effects this size. You will get a false read and act on it.
- **An affiliate program** — a 30% cut of $19 is $5.70, under most payout floors.
- **Hashtag research.** Instagram capped posts at five hashtags (Dec 2025), removed hashtag-following (Dec 2024), and Mosseri said twice in 2025 they do not improve reach. Use zero to two topic tags and no tools.
- **Countdown timers, "only 3 left", fake purchase popups.** Resetting timers are a named FTC dark pattern, and scarcity on an infinitely copyable file is self-evidently false to an audience already scanning for scams.
- **Any "Gumroad statistics 2026" figure.** Gumroad does not publish per-product sales; those numbers are inferred from public rating counts times an undisclosed multiplier. Do not set a target from one.

## 5. Sales page structure (Gumroad)

Write it in this order. Most people bury the point; do not.

1. **One-line promise** — *"It tells you which two things to abandon."* Lead with the **cut list**. Do NOT lead with "sized to the hours you actually have": [Grind 75](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/grind75) takes hours-per-week and weeks and outputs an ordered plan, free, written by the author of Blind 75. You cannot win that framing and using it invites the comparison.
2. **The problem, in their words** — *"You have five hours a week and a plan built for fifteen. Something has to go — and nothing will tell you what."*
3. **What it does** — four bullets: **names what you must cut** · reads your resume locally, nothing uploaded · 20-question diagnostic that reweights your plan · one payment, no account, works offline forever.
3b. **Name Grind 75 yourself**, in a comparison row, and say it is free and better than you at DSA scheduling. Honesty here is cheaper than being caught, and it converts your biggest competitor into a credibility signal.
4. **Three screenshots** — the diagnostic result with section bars, a generated week, the progress view.
5. **What it is not** — *"Not a job guarantee. Not a course. Not 400 videos you will never watch."* Naming this raises trust and cuts refunds.
6. **Privacy line** — *"Runs entirely in your browser. Your resume never leaves your device. Works offline."* This is a genuine differentiator; say it loudly.
7. **Price + what you get** — $19 / ₹999, one HTML file, yours forever, no subscription, no login.
8. **Refund policy** — 14 days, no questions, in the first person: *"Email me and I refund it. No form, no questions, and you keep the file."*
9. **Then stop editing the page.** No changes until 300 unique visitors have landed. You cannot A/B test at your traffic — resolving effects this size needs ~10,000 visitors *per variant*. Build the trust assets once and spend every hour after that on traffic.

**Objections and the honest answer:**

| They think | You say |
|---|---|
| "$19 for an HTML file?" | It is a tool, not a document. It scores you, reweights the plan around your gaps, holds your progress, and names what you have to drop — then keeps working with your Wi-Fi off, forever, with no subscription. |
| "Isn't Grind 75 free?" | Yes, and it is better than me at DSA scheduling — I say so on the page. It won't read your resume, won't score you, and won't tell you what to abandon when the plan doesn't fit. Use both. |
| "Can't I just ask ChatGPT?" | You can, and it is a reasonable place to start. The difference is this scores you first and holds the result, instead of you re-prompting until it sounds right and then losing it tomorrow. |
| "Will this get me a job?" | No, and anyone promising that is lying. It tells you what to work on and keeps you honest about the hours. The work is still yours. |
| "Is my resume safe?" | It never leaves your laptop. There is no server. Turn off your Wi-Fi and it still works — try it. |

## 6. Payments and delivery

**Set up exactly ONE rail.** A dual-rail geo-detected checkout is build time spent before you have a single buyer. Let your first ten conversations decide which.

- **Razorpay** — if your buyers are Indian. 2% + GST on the fee, UPI support, direct payout to an Indian bank account. Materially cheaper than any foreign merchant-of-record.
- **Gumroad** — if your buyers are global. Simplest setup, handles global sales tax as merchant of record, 10% + $0.50.
- **Not Instamojo** for this product — its own help centre prices digital goods well above physical, penalising exactly what you sell.
- **Price:** $19 globally, ₹999 in India. Keep 9-endings in both. Note honestly that ₹999 is a judgement, not a measurement — there is no credible data on Indian conversion at this price, which is what the first ten DMs are for.
- **Do not withdraw in small amounts.** Fixed per-payout fees make cashing out your first $40 the most expensive habit available to you. Let it accumulate.
- **Do not register for GST voluntarily** "to look professional." Two notifications (10/2017-IT and 65/2017-CT) exempt you below ₹20 lakh aggregate turnover including exports. Registering converts zero compliance into permanent monthly filings on a product with almost no input credit to recover.
- **Delivery** — the ZIP from `dist/`. Include the README so the first thing they read tells them to open `app/index.html`.

**Test your own checkout at full price before launch.** A broken download is the most expensive bug you can ship.

## 7. What "working" looks like — decide these now

Set the bar before you have feelings invested in the outcome.

Set the bar low enough to be honest. The research puts **~85–90% odds of under $100/month for the first three months** — reasoned from Gumroad's own July 2025 distribution, where 8,507 of 37,006 already-earning creators cleared $100 in a month. You start below that population: no audience, 3 h/week, and a price band where the only dated post-mortems report zero and single-digit sales.

| Month | Realistic | If you miss it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **0 sales.** Audience and conversations only. | Nothing. This is the expected value, not a warning sign. |
| 2 | 5–15 | Check the *audience* before the copy. Run the 0.5% gate. |
| 3 | 20–40 cumulative | Your format is not landing. Copy what demonstrably works in the niche instead of inventing. |
| 4 | 40–80 cumulative | If under 20 total **and** your interview prep has slipped — stop and reassess honestly. |

**Do not measure the app monthly.** Both verifiable successes in this niche took 1–3 years of publishing before the product landed. Monthly measurement of a thing that compounds annually just manufactures the feeling of failure. Measure the *publishing streak* monthly and the revenue quarterly.

**The real decision at month 4:** is 3 h/week better spent here, or on the prep that leads to a ₹10–25L role? Both is fine while the content *is* your study log. The moment the business starts stealing prep hours, it is costing you more than it earns — and near-term money should come from the session, not the file.
